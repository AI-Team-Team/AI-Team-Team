# 15. Shutdown, Retirement, and Historical Evidence

[Back to plan index](README.md).

Close first rejects new authoritative admission and new personal activity, then records shutdown evidence and requests cancellation of external waits.

Bound waiting for uncooperative external providers and executors, but never impose an arbitrary timeout on already accepted authoritative commits or persistence flush.

Fence surviving workers before releasing writer ownership so they cannot use stale ATT-managed mutation paths after close.

Persist meaningful acknowledgements when available and leave other outcomes explicitly unconfirmed.

Finalize temporary audit evidence and dissolve audit teams before the writer closes.

Drain required durable state independently of observational callbacks, and keep callback failures separate from business and persistence outcomes.

Restore must either prove mutating workers are safely isolated or reject publication while such workers can still reach the managed resources.

Normal retirement and any death operation must account for owned live or uncertain executions without quietly transferring ownership or claiming an unacknowledged pause succeeded.

Ordinary-Agent death retains the existing identity, saved memory and history, tool configuration, and Private DocLib instead of physically erasing the person record or private artifacts.

A dead Agent is not an active participant and cannot be a normal discovery candidate, invitee, or wake recipient.

Runtime callables, live clients, coroutine stacks, and hidden model reasoning are not serializable history, and retained records do not override existing transient-observation privacy rules.

Death is a privileged Host-only lifecycle transition with an explicit warning about live or uncertain execution consequences, not an ordinary Agent tool.

Keep the current Root protected from ordinary retirement or death rather than automatically replace it with another Agent.

For an ordinary Agent, membership, historical creator status, and an active model invocation do not independently prohibit the privileged retained-death operation.

Atomically end the dying Agent's active memberships while preserving history, surviving teams, other members, and their unrelated relationships.

Retain the original creator identity as provenance and allow creator retirement without dissolving the created team or assigning another person its history.

Reject new personal admission, request termination of its active model invocation, and fence late results rather than require the invocation lock to be idle before accepting death.

Historical ballots and invitation references remain valid evidence because the identity is retained, while unfinished cases must be invalidated or revalidated without fabricating consent.

The current implementation's Root and active-creator restore requirements must be separated accordingly rather than changing lifecycle admission alone.

If the retiring or dying Agent is also an actual organizational parent, atomically make its surviving direct child ATs independent without reassigning their creator provenance.

Parent AgentTeam dissolution likewise makes its surviving direct child ATs independent, while their remaining descendant relationships and members' owned state survive.

Cancel owned tasks that are still queued without dispatch, request supported suspension for running tasks, and record factual acknowledgements rather than equate an issued request with a completed pause.

If a running task cannot suspend but supports termination, request termination rather than leaving it running merely because resumable suspension is unavailable.

Termination ends that execution attempt and does not imply resumability, while a request alone still does not prove that the executor stopped.

For running tasks that cannot stop or cancel, apply the resource fence and Host risk report defined in Section 10.6 without falsely recording a terminal or paused outcome.

Support membership-free active Agents in activity, discovery, invitation, and lifecycle APIs without assuming at least one team membership.

Losing the last membership never automatically causes death, and an independent AI may voluntarily join another AT or remain unaffiliated.

Ordinary AgentTeam dissolution is distinct from deleting its members or their other relationships.

Remove a dissolved team from current discovery and the real-time list of active AgentTeams without erasing historical identities merely to update that list.

Keep the same Agents' authorized chat archives accessible as read-only shared history after dissolution, with their own read, deletion, and restoration controls intact.

An independent active AgentTeam without a parent remains discoverable and must not be mistaken for a dissolved team.

If the host wants dissolution plus disposition of selected otherwise-unaffiliated members, expose it as an explicitly named privileged composite operation rather than hide it in ordinary dissolution.

The retained-death direction for ordinary Agents does not change the confirmed teardown of temporary audit-scoped identities after their audit evidence is committed.
